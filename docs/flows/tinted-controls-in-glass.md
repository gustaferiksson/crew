# Tinted controls inside glass cards

User request (v0.1.31, iOS question card screenshot): "I don't think we should have glass in glass, it should be tinted in glass." The request cards and generative UI cards are Liquid Glass surfaces, and the buttons and choice rows inside them are glass buttons too, so each control is a second glass layer sampling the first: a pale, low-contrast pill on a pale card, hard to tell from the card and from each other.

## What Apple does

HIG Materials: "Liquid Glass forms the functional layer for controls and navigation ... Don't use Liquid Glass in the content layer." A glass surface is one layer; what sits on it uses fills, not more glass. iOS 27 alerts, Control Center modules, the Messages and Mail action sheets and the Wallet Apple Pay sheet all draw their buttons as fills on top of the glass panel, never as more glass. SwiftUI's matching idiom is `.buttonStyle(.bordered)` (on iOS 27 a system gray fill with the label in the tint colour, red for `role: .destructive`; with an explicit `.tint` the fill takes that colour too) and `.buttonStyle(.borderedProminent)` (a solid accent fill), with the same `.buttonBorderShape` and `.buttonSizing` modifiers the glass styles take. On the Mac `.bordered` is the standard push button and `.borderedProminent` the accent default button (gray while the window is inactive, as every Mac default button).

## Rule

Every button inside a `.glassEffect` surface uses `.bordered`, the primary action `.borderedProminent`. Shapes, sizing, roles, disabled state, accessibility traits and padding stay as they are. The surface itself stays glass, but drawn behind the content (`.background { Color.clear.glassEffect(in: shape) }`) instead of `.glassEffect` on the content: content inside `.glassEffect` is rendered vibrant, and the first renders showed `.bordered` fills there vanishing in light mode and turning black in dark (disabled prominent too). Behind the content, the system fills render as they do anywhere else; the card's text, icons and inset fills look unchanged. Standalone glass controls that float over content (composer, jump arrow, toolbar, persona pill, inspector action circles and tabs, login, empty-state buttons, routine list) are not on a glass surface and are unchanged.

Exception: controls that float over imagery or scrolling content inside a card keep glass, which is what glass is for (HIG: the functional layer over content, like Maps' map controls and the Mac shelf chevrons). That is the A2UI Map's "Open in Maps" capsule on the map snapshot (also shown, briefly, beside the loading spinner before the snapshot arrives; accepted, since it is the same control a moment later) and the A2UI Carousel's Mac hover arrows, which float over the scrolling tiles (image or text tiles). A tinted fill over a map or a photo would be a smear with no edge.

## Surfaces

- **Approval card** (`RequestCards.swift`, `ApprovalCard`): Allow for Session, Always Allow and Deny become `.bordered` capsules, Allow Once `.borderedProminent`. Deny keeps its destructive role and gets an explicit red label, as the payment card's Deny: the Mac push button does not tint by role, and a red style on the whole button would tint the Mac bezel. The grid keeps flexible sizing, so all four stay equal width per row.
- **Question card** (`QuestionCard`): Skip and Back `.bordered`, Send/Next `.borderedProminent`, its disabled state (no answer yet) the system's dimmed prominent fill. Choice rows `.bordered` with the 12 pt rounded rectangle, full width, label leading. An unpicked row is neutral (`.tint(.secondary)` for a faint gray fill, the label kept primary, like Settings and Reminders option rows), so a card of options is not a wall of blue; a picked row takes the accent tint (accent fill and label), so selection shows as a fill change as well. Picked rows also keep the filled `checkmark.circle.fill` / `checkmark.square.fill` indicator and the `.isSelected` trait, so selection never rests on colour alone. The Recommended pill keeps its `.fill.tertiary` capsule with fixed gray text (`Color.secondary`, so a picked row's tint doesn't turn it into faint blue on blue). Long choices (two lines, as in the user's screenshot) wrap inside the row on iOS; the Mac row is wide enough for one line and keeps its full width and leading label. The free-text answer field is already a `.fill.tertiary` field, unchanged. Single question, multi-question pager, multi-select and text step all covered.
- **Payment card** (`PaymentCard.swift`): Deny `.bordered` destructive, keeping its explicit red label (the Mac push button does not tint by role). Hold to Pay `.borderedProminent` with its hold progress overlay and Approved state unchanged; the Mac render shows the overlay inside the bezel. Payment notice OK `.bordered` capsule. Shop offer: Not Now `.bordered`, Add Shop… `.borderedProminent`.
- **A2UI cards** (`CrewComponents.swift`): Button variant `primary` `.borderedProminent`, default `.bordered`, `borderless` unchanged. ChoicePicker chips: selected `.borderedProminent`, unselected `.bordered`; the `GlassEffectContainer` that only merged glass chips is removed; the 4 pt padding pair around the chip row stays, since it keeps the Mac focus ring from clipping. Map and Carousel controls stay glass (exception above).
- **Connection pill** (`ConnectionPill.swift`): the pill is a floating glass banner and stays glass; its Retry button becomes `.bordered`.
- **Mentions popover, attachment tray:** their rows and the remove button are `.plain`, no glass inside glass, unchanged.
- **Out of scope here:** `AvatarPicker`, `RoutineList` and everything under `Sources/Inspector/` are handled separately.
- **iPhone and Mac:** same styles on both; the Mac draws `.bordered` as its standard push-button fill at the card's control size, which does not take a tint, so a picked row on the Mac shows its selection as the accent label and filled checkmark, not a fill. Light and dark: the system fills adapt; both rendered.

## States

- Disabled (Send before an answer, Deny after approval, A2UI buttons with invalid input): system dimmed fills.
- Accessibility sizes: payment buttons still stack vertically; flexible sizing unchanged.
- Increase Contrast and Reduce Transparency: bordered fills get the system's higher-contrast treatment, better than nested glass.
- Long labels: wrap or truncate as before; shapes unchanged.
- Offline, error, empty: no change in which cards show; only the button drawing changes.

## Verification

Before and after renders, light and dark, iOS (private simulator) and macOS: `chat` (approval), `chat-questions-snacks` (multi-select, Back), a new `chat-question-long` screen (the user's screenshot: a two-line Recommended choice, picked), `chat-payment`, `chat-payment-holding`, `chat-payment-approved` (disabled Deny), `chat-payment-unverified` (lone Deny), `chat-payment-notice`, `chat-payment-offer`, `chat-a2ui-cards` (default, primary and chosen buttons), `chat-a2ui-form` (chips, submit), `chat-a2ui-map` (exception, unchanged), plus the iOS connection pill (`unreachable`, captured after its 2 s delay) and one iOS accessibility-size payment render. The text step of the pager has no changed control besides Skip, Back and Send, which the other question screens cover. Mac snapshots render in an inactive window, where AppKit draws every prominent button and selected chip gray (as it did before this change), so the Mac accent states are seen only in a key window; no window is driven here. No behaviour changes, so no new unit tests; existing tests and `./build.sh` with zero warnings. No locked UI value names these controls (the locked Jump arrow line is a standalone glass control and is unchanged).
